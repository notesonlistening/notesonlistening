## Moved
*The emotional side. What happens between you and the music, alone.*

- Why we listen to the same song 47 times when we're sad
- Why we cry at a single harmony
- The breakup playlist — therapy or a way to stay stuck?
- Music and anxiety — how sound calms the nervous system
- "Matilda is my therapy" — what that says about us
- Musical nostalgia — why a song from 2009 can undo you in 30 seconds
- The frisson — why some people feel it and others don't
- Crying without knowing why — emotional response without memory anchor
- Music and grief — how we listen differently after a loss
- Active vs passive listening — what changes when you really listen
- The end of an album — why the last track does something particular
- Rediscovering a song — hearing it years later and it means something else
- The involuntary physical response — the body before the brain
- The song you can no longer listen to — not nostalgia, the association that blocks
- **The cover that rewrites the original** — when a version changes how you hear the source forever. The intimacy that reveals what the original didn't show. *(→ see also Never Met, Sound of Self)*
- **Fandoms non-anglophones** — loving an artist in a language you don't speak. Bad Bunny without Spanish, Måneskin without Italian, K-pop without Korean. Does not understanding the words change the connection — or purify it? What music actually is before language gets to it. *(→ anchored here: this is fundamentally about the relationship to music before words)*
- **Why a song becomes a favorite** — not the frisson, not the MEAM. The song that resists wear, that keeps returning. What makes that happen neurologically and emotionally.

## Never Met
*The parasocial. The obsession. Artists we've never met.*

- On loving an artist who doesn't know you exist
- Romantic feelings for someone who doesn't know you exist — effects on mental health
- The blurry line between admiration and obsession
- When parasocial attachment becomes too heavy
- Artists' responsibility toward fans in distress
- Artists who deliberately manipulate their fans' attachment
- Grieving an artist you never met — Cobain, Winehouse, Lil Peep
- The cost of being publicly vulnerable as an artist
- The healthy parasocial — when attachment is genuinely beneficial
- Social media and the illusion of access — Instagram, TikTok, simulated proximity
- The fan who becomes an artist — how obsession transforms into creation
- When the artist disappoints — the parasocial breakup, disenchantment
- The dead vs living artist — why we attach differently
- **Fan fiction and parasocial creativity** — writing a person you invented from someone you've never met. The most pure form of creative parasocial attachment. Barely treated seriously.
- **Leaks and their impact** — the particular intimacy of having something the artist didn't intend to give you. Medicine as case study: the song that became cult precisely because it was never official. Fan cultures around protecting vs spreading leaks. What we believe we're entitled to have. *(→ see also Off Record)*
- **The cover as conversation between artists** — a way of saying something to someone you admire, across distance or time. *(→ see also Moved)*

## Your Brain on Music
*The science. Dopamine, memory, frisson. Never cold.*

> Note on "science": used in the broad sense — the opposite of emotion and the irrational. Neurology, sociology, psychology. Never cold.

- Musical frisson — why some people feel it and others don't
- Involuntary memory — why a song instantly brings back a moment
- Music and neurodivergence / ADHD — a particularly intense relationship
- Music and physical pain — palliative care, childbirth
- Music and dissociation
- Why certain sounds soothe — ASMR, binaural beats, white noise *(watch: risks sounding wellness)*
- Babies and music — before language, before consciousness
- The earworm — why a song gets stuck, the neurological mechanism
- Alzheimer and music — remembering songs when everything else is gone
- Why certain chord progressions always work
- Silence — what happens neurologically in the absence of music
- Music therapy — demystifying without pretending to be an expert *(the parenthesis in the original title signals hesitation — resolve the angle before writing)*
- Why we listen less well as we age — brain plasticity and taste
- **Babies, music, and language acquisition** — babies acquire language partly through prosodic features: rhythm, melody, pitch — exactly what music is. Music may precede language in evolution. The baby hears the song before the meaning. Connects directly with Alzheimer as counterpoint: the beginning and the end answering each other. Possible issue angle: *Before Words*.

## Off Record
*Artists writing from their darkest places.*

- Artists who write about their depression — what it means to fans to feel seen
- The cost of public vulnerability for an artist
- The breakup album — what it costs to make your most intimate pain public
- When the artist gets better — why fans are sometimes disappointed the music changes
- The myth of the tortured genius — do we romanticise artists' suffering
- Artists who refuse to explain — the right to silence on what they created
- Writing about someone without their permission — the song as public settling of scores
- The artist who recovers too fast — when the audience can't keep up with their healing
- **Posthumous** — moved to Issues as a candidate for a full issue. See issues file. *(Mac Miller / Circles, Juice WRLD, X, Lil Peep — each case different. Who decides what gets released. The unfinished that should stay unfinished. Grief exploited commercially or given as a gift.)*
- **The image contract** — what an artist chooses to show, what that promises the fan, what happens when it was a construction. Queerbaiting as the most charged example: manipulating identity-based attachment without ever confirming. The "relatable" artist, the "authentic" persona built from scratch, simulated intimacy. The question: not marketing — what we believed we'd received, and the betrayal when we hadn't. *(reformulated from "The making of an image")*
- **Leaks from the artist's side** — the other half of the Never Met entry. What it means to have something surface that you didn't intend to give. *(→ see also Never Met)*
